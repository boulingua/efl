## Lead-in story

Final mediation Unit. The source is a short fictional policy-style passage written for this exercise. Its sample size, findings and study period are invented; it is not a federal report or evidence about German education. Imagine an English-speaking comparative-education researcher as your addressee. The task is to convey only what this passage says, including its uncertainty and limits. Do not invent the contents of a longer report.

## 1. Activate {#activate}
**Audit scan.** Label the sentences that describe the study, method, findings and limitation. Underline a hedge and circle the German institutional terms. The passage has no separate executive summary or methods section; do not infer material that is absent.

## 2. Input {#input}
### Source — fictional policy-style passage

*Der folgende fiktive Bericht untersucht die Entwicklung der Bildungsgerechtigkeit in Deutschland zwischen 2010 und 2025 und bezieht sich auf eine bundesweite Stichprobe von 1,2 Millionen Schülerinnen und Schülern. Methodisch nutzen wir mehrebenenanalytische Modelle. Die Ergebnisse legen nahe, dass die Korrelation zwischen sozio-ökonomischem Hintergrund und PISA-Ergebnis in den Stadtstaaten Berlin, Hamburg und Bremen leicht abgenommen hat, in den Flächenländern hingegen stabil geblieben ist. Bildungspolitische Implikationen diskutieren wir vorsichtig. Eine kausale Interpretation der Korrelationen ist auf Basis der vorliegenden Daten nicht möglich.*

### Mediation conventions for policy texts

**German-specific institutional terms:** *Stadtstaaten* (German city-states — Berlin, Hamburg, Bremen), *Flächenländer* (German federal states other than the three city-states), *Bildungsgerechtigkeit* (educational equity / fairness), *Mehrebenen-analyse* (multilevel modelling).

**Hedge preservation:** *legen nahe* → *suggest*; *kausale Interpretation … nicht möglich* → *causal inference is not possible from the available data*.

**Source references:** preserve the source’s reference to *PISA*. It contains no citation, link or NEPS finding; do not add one. Make the passage’s fictional status clear in your introduction.

## 3. Practise {#practise}
### Niveau E — controlled

1. Match German term → English: *Stadtstaaten* → ?, *Flächenländer* → ?, *Bildungsgerechtigkeit* → ?, *Mehrebenenanalyse* → ?
2. Decide for the OECD addressee: keep + explain in brackets / paraphrase / drop entirely — *Stadtstaaten*, *PISA*, *Mehrebenenanalyse*.

### Niveau E — productive

3. Build a 6-sentence English mediation of the source above.

{{< callout type="tip" title="Answer key" collapse="true" >}}
**Controlled.** 1. German city-states / federal states other than the three city-states / educational equity / multilevel modelling.
2. *Stadtstaaten*: keep + 5-word note. *PISA*: keep without note. *Mehrebenenanalyse*: keep + 3-word note for non-statisticians.

**Productive.** 3. Open.
{{< /callout >}}

## 4. Produce {#apply}
**Final mediation, six to eight sentences.** Write for the comparative-education researcher. Identify the source as a fictional exercise, preserve the study period, sample, method, regional comparison and limit on causal inference. Explain an institutional term if the reader needs it. Choose reporting verbs for accuracy; there is no quota. Do not add claims about robustness, funding, data access, later research or other datasets.

### Sample

{{< details role="key" summary="Source-faithful model" >}}
This fictional policy-style passage describes an investigation of educational equity in Germany between 2010 and 2025. It states that the study uses a nationwide sample of 1.2 million school students and multilevel models. Its findings suggest that the correlation between socioeconomic background and PISA results decreased slightly in Berlin, Hamburg and Bremen, Germany’s three city-states. In the other federal states, the correlation remained stable. The authors discuss policy implications cautiously. They state that the available data do not allow a causal interpretation of these correlations. These statements belong to the invented exercise and should not be cited as research findings.
{{< /details >}}

- [ ] I can mediate a short fictional policy-style text in six to eight faithful English sentences.
- [ ] I can preserve hedges, the causal limitation and necessary institutional explanations.
- [ ] I can choose accurate reporting verbs without adding unsupported claims.

**Differentiation — support (below Niveau E).**

Build your mediation from these frames — fill each gap and choose reporting verbs that match the source:

- Opener: `Here is a mediation of a fictional policy-style passage on educational equity.`
- Finding + hedge: `The report examines ______ between 2010 and 2025, and the findings suggest that ______.`
- Reporting verbs (choose by meaning): *state · suggest · note*. Avoid *confirm* when the source only suggests a finding. Example: `The authors stress that ______.`
- Institutional note bracket: `in the ______ (city-states: Berlin, Hamburg, Bremen)` and `in the ______ (the other German federal states).`
- Central hedge to keep: `The authors add that a causal interpretation is not possible from the available data.`

**Extension (above Niveau E / Oberstufe).**

After completing your six-to-eight-sentence mediation, write a 150-word critical commentary in which you defend your three most contestable choices: one German term you kept untranslated, one hedge you preserved word-for-word, and one reporting verb you selected over a neutral alternative. Argue explicitly how each choice either safeguards or subtly shifts the report's original stance for an OECD reader, and identify the single point at which honest mediation and reader-friendliness came into genuine tension. Then propose how a British broadsheet science editor would have mediated that same point differently, and what that contrast reveals about register as an ethical, not merely stylistic, decision.

## Common pitfalls

- Don't translate institutional terms (*Stadtstaaten*) — keep + explain.
- *kausale Interpretation nicht möglich* is the report's central hedge; preserve it.
- Distinguish source claims from your own interpretation; this fictional passage is not evidence for a real policy decision.
