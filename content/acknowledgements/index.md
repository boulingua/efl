This site stands on a long lineage of teachers, writers, and tool-
builders. The list below is incomplete by design — it names the
direct influences that shape the materials, not every book read
along the way.

## Didactics and pedagogy

- **Penny Ur** — for the discipline of staged tasks and
  classroom-tested rationale. <https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/a-course-in-english-language-teaching/>
- **Jim Scrivener** — for the teacher's-eye view of what really
  happens in the room. <https://www.macmillaneducation.com>
- **Scott Thornbury** — for vocabulary, lexical approach, and the
  habit of starting from learner language. <https://scottthornbury.wordpress.com/>
- **Paul Nation** — for the data behind vocabulary teaching.
  <https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/about/staff/paul-nation>
- **David Crystal** — for keeping language description honest and
  alive. <https://www.davidcrystal.com>
- **Council of Europe — CEFR Companion Volume** — for the Can-do
  scaffolding that frames every Unit's learning objectives.
  <https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages>

## Structural inspiration

The dual-format scaffold (one source, HTML + slides) is adapted from
public Quarto course templates and from the long tradition of
literate-programming course websites in the open-source community.

## Personal

To the colleagues who tested half-finished worksheets in their
classrooms, and to every student who said "this doesn't work, try
something else": this site is what it is because of you.
