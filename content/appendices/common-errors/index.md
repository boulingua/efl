L1-German learners of English produce a recognisable family of
errors. This page is a quick-reference. Per-year group ("Klassenstufe") entries
will be expanded as Units land in Phases 3–4.

## Word order

**Problem.** German verb-second pulled into English: *"Today is the
weather nice"*, *"After school I have done my homework"*.

**Strategy.** Drill SVO with explicit time-place adverbial slots.
Picture-based prompts (Kl. 5–7) and sentence-frame writing (Kl.
8–10) work better than rule explanation alone.

## Tense and aspect

**Problem.** *Present perfect vs. simple past* maps poorly onto
*Perfekt vs. Präteritum*. Learners overuse present perfect for
events with a clear past time-marker (*"I have seen the film
yesterday"*).

**Strategy.** Pair the present perfect with state-now contexts and
"since/for" timelines from Kl. 7. Drill simple-past with overt
past-time markers (*yesterday*, *last week*, *in 2020*).

## Articles

**Problem.** German learners under- and over-use *the*. They drop
the indefinite article in profession statements (*"She is teacher"*)
and overuse *the* with abstract nouns (*"The life is hard"*).

**Strategy.** Teach article use as a two-step decision: (1) is it
specific or generic? (2) is it count or non-count? Use authentic
short texts and have learners highlight every article and justify
it.

## False friends

A small but high-frequency set causes most issues:

- *eventually* ≠ *eventuell* (eventually = "in the end" vs.
  eventuell = "perhaps")
- *actually* ≠ *aktuell* (actually = "in fact" vs. aktuell =
  "current/up-to-date")
- *to become* ≠ *bekommen* (become = "turn into" vs. bekommen =
  "to get/receive")
- *sympathetic* ≠ *sympathisch* (sympathetic = "compassionate" vs.
  sympathisch = "likeable")
- *brave* ≠ *brav* (brave = "courageous" vs. brav = "well-behaved")
- *gift* ≠ *Gift* (gift = "present" vs. Gift = "poison")

**Strategy.** Cluster these in vocabulary slides and revisit
across Klassenstufen — false friends never go away on their own.

## Pronunciation

**Problem.** Final voicing distinction (*bag/back*, *had/hat*),
*th* sounds (*think/thank*), and word stress on cognates (*ho-tel*
vs. *Ho-tel*).

**Strategy.** Slide-deck speaker notes flag cognate stress and
provide minimal-pair drills at points where cognates appear in the
target text.

## Capitalisation, punctuation, quoting

**Problem.** German rules carried over: capitalised nouns
(*"the Teacher"*), comma before *that* in subordinate clauses, and
German-style quotation marks („…").

**Strategy.** Build a one-page house style for every year group ("Klassenstufe")
and refer to it in writing tasks. The style appears in
[Writing rubrics](/appendices/writing-rubrics/).
